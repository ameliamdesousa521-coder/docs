The Medical Index is built on the trust of a community that relies on the platform for accurate, relevant, and up-to-date medical research. Clear submission guidelines protect that trust by ensuring every paper on the platform meets a consistent standard of quality and relevance. Reading these guidelines before you submit saves time during review and helps your paper reach readers faster.

## Content Requirements

Every submission must satisfy the following criteria to be accepted:

<Accordion title="Peer-reviewed or clearly labeled as a preprint">
  The paper must have undergone formal peer review through a recognized journal or conference, **or** be explicitly marked as a preprint (see [Preprints and Non-Peer-Reviewed Work](#preprints-and-non-peer-reviewed-work) below). Unpublished manuscripts that are neither peer-reviewed nor labeled as preprints are not accepted.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Relevant to human health, medicine, or biomedical research">
  All submissions must directly relate to human health outcomes, clinical medicine, biomedical science, public health, or adjacent fields such as medical technology and health policy. Research limited to non-human subjects is only accepted when there is a clear and explicit translational relevance to human health.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Includes an abstract">
  Every submission must contain a complete abstract. The abstract is used for indexing, search, and weekly digest previews. If you are submitting via DOI and the abstract is not fetched automatically, paste it into the metadata form manually.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Lists at least one author">
  The paper must attribute authorship to at least one named individual. Anonymous submissions are not accepted.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Is not a duplicate of an existing submission">
  Do not submit a paper that is already listed on The Medical Index. Before submitting, search the platform to confirm that your paper has not been submitted previously — by you or by another user.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Preprints are labeled as such">
  If your paper has not yet completed peer review, you must select the **Preprint** tag when filling in the journal or publication field. Labeling preprints correctly lets readers make informed decisions about the evidence level they are reviewing.
</Accordion>

## What Gets Rejected

The editorial team will decline submissions that fall into the following categories:

- **Non-medical content** — papers outside the scope of human health, medicine, or biomedical research (e.g., purely veterinary studies with no human translational component, engineering papers unrelated to health).
- **Duplicate submissions** — papers already listed on The Medical Index, regardless of which account submitted them first.
- **Promotional or commercial material** — product white papers, sponsored content, advertorials, or any document whose primary purpose is to promote a commercial product or service rather than report original research.
- **Papers without author attribution** — any submission that does not name at least one identifiable author.
- **Unreadable or incomplete files** — scanned image-only PDFs, corrupted files, or submissions with critical metadata missing (e.g., no title, no abstract).
- **Unlabeled preprints** — papers not yet peer-reviewed that are submitted without the preprint designation.

If your submission is rejected, you will receive an email explaining the reason. You are welcome to revise and resubmit once the issue has been addressed.

## Preprints and Non-Peer-Reviewed Work

The Medical Index accepts preprints because timely access to emerging research benefits the community, even before formal peer review is complete. However, preprints must be handled transparently:

- Select **Preprint** as the publication type when completing your submission metadata.
- Preprints are displayed with a clearly visible **Preprint** badge on the platform so readers can immediately identify them.
- Readers can filter out preprints from search results and specialty feeds if they prefer to see only peer-reviewed content.
- If a preprint you submitted is later published in a peer-reviewed journal, contact us to update the listing with the final publication details.

Preprints sourced from recognized servers such as medRxiv, bioRxiv, and SSRN are commonly submitted and accepted, provided all other content requirements are met.

## Attribution and Copyright

By submitting a paper to The Medical Index, you confirm that:

- You are the author, or you have the author's explicit permission to share the work on this platform.
- Sharing the paper on The Medical Index does not violate any publisher embargo, license agreement, or copyright restriction.
- The information you provide in the metadata accurately represents the paper and its authors.

**The Medical Index does not claim ownership of any submitted content.** All intellectual property rights remain with the original authors and publishers. The platform acts solely as an index and discovery layer — it does not modify, republish, or redistribute papers beyond making them searchable and accessible within the platform.

If you are unsure whether your paper's license or publisher agreement permits sharing on a third-party platform, check the journal's author rights policy or consult the [SHERPA/RoMEO database](https://v2.sherpa.ac.uk/romeo/) before submitting.

<Note>
  All submissions go through an editorial review before appearing on The Medical Index. Submitting a paper does not guarantee publication. The review team checks every submission against these guidelines and will contact you if changes are needed.
</Note>
