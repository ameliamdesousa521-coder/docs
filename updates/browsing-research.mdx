The Medical Index gives you direct access to a growing library of peer-reviewed medical research, all in one place. The research browsing experience is designed to help you move quickly — whether you're conducting a focused literature search, keeping tabs on a specific author's output, or simply scanning for new developments in your field. Powerful search, flexible filters, and specialty tags work together to surface the papers most relevant to you with minimal effort.

## Using the Research Feed

The default research feed shows you the newest papers added to The Medical Index, listed in reverse chronological order so the most recently published work always appears at the top. As you scroll through the feed, each entry displays the paper title, authors, journal name, specialty tags, and the date it was added to the platform.

You can adjust how the feed is sorted using the **Sort by** dropdown at the top of the feed:

- **Newest** — displays papers in the order they were added to the platform, most recent first. This is the default view.
- **Most Viewed** — surfaces papers that have attracted the most reads from users on the platform, useful for identifying high-interest research.
- **Most Relevant** — when you have specialty preferences set in your profile, this sort order weights papers that match your interests more heavily.

## Searching for Research

The search bar at the top of the research feed lets you find specific papers quickly. The Medical Index supports several search modes to help you narrow your results.

### Keyword Search

Type any term into the search bar to search across paper titles, abstracts, and keyword tags. Use specific clinical terms or disease names for the most precise results. Search results update as you type, and matching terms are highlighted in each result.

### Author Search

To find papers by a specific researcher, type their name into the search bar. Switch the search mode to **Author** using the filter pill below the search bar to limit results to papers where that person appears as an author or co-author.

### Filtering by Date Range

Narrow your search to a specific time window using the **Date Range** filter. Click the calendar icon next to the search bar, enter a start date and an end date, and the feed will display only papers published within that range. This is especially useful when researching the evolution of findings over time or looking for the most recent work on a topic.

## Filtering by Specialty

Every paper on The Medical Index is tagged with one or more medical specialties. Use the **Specialty** filter panel on the left side of the research feed to limit results to one or more disciplines. You can select multiple specialties simultaneously to cast a wider net or compare findings across fields. For a full guide to how specialties are organized on the platform, see [Research by Specialty](/updates/specialties).

## Viewing a Paper

Click any paper title in the feed to open its detail page. The paper detail page brings together everything you need to evaluate and engage with a piece of research:

- **Abstract** — a structured summary of the paper's background, methods, results, and conclusions.
- **Authors** — a list of all authors with links to their author profile pages, where you can browse their other publications on the platform.
- **Journal** — the name of the publishing journal and the original publication date.
- **Upload date** — the date the paper was added to The Medical Index.
- **Specialty tags** — the disciplines the paper has been filed under, each clickable to browse related research.
- **Full text** — when the full text of a paper is available, a **Read Full Paper** button appears on the detail page. Availability depends on open-access status and publisher agreements.

<Tip>
  To save papers you want to revisit later, click the **Bookmark** icon on any paper card in the feed or on the paper detail page. Access all your saved papers at any time from the **My Library** section of your account.
</Tip>
